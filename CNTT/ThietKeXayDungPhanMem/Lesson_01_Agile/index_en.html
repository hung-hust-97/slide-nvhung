POPULAR AGILE FRAMEWORKS

Scrum

  • Most popular framework (>70% of Agile projects)
  • Divides projects into 2-4 week Sprints
  • Has 3 clear roles: PO, SM, Dev Team
  • Best for: complex projects with frequently changing requirements

Kanban

  • Visualizes work through Kanban boards
  • No fixed sprints, continuous flow
  • Limits WIP (Work In Progress)
  • Best for: maintenance, support, DevOps

Extreme Programming (XP)

  • Focuses on excellent engineering practices
  • Test-Driven Development (TDD)
  • Pair Programming, Continuous Integration
  • Best for: projects requiring high code quality

Lean Development

  • Waste elimination
  • Optimize the entire value stream
  • Defer decisions as late as possible
  • Best for: startups, MVP development

SCRUM HISTORY

1986

Origins

Takeuchi & Nonaka introduced the term "Scrum" in Harvard Business Review, inspired by rugby team formations

1995

Formalization

Jeff Sutherland & Ken Schwaber defined Scrum at the OOPSLA conference, after successfully applying it at Easel Corporation (1993)

2001

Agile Manifesto

Scrum became closely tied to the Agile Manifesto, establishing its leading role in agile software development

2020

Scrum Guide Update

The latest version emphasizes simplicity, removes rigid rules, and is more suitable for cross-industry application

THREE PILLARS OF SCRUM (EMPIRICISM)

Scrum is based on Empiricism — knowledge comes from experience and real-world observation:

🔍 Transparency

  • Information is shared openly with all stakeholders
  • Everyone understands the current status and project goals
  • E.g.: Scrum/Kanban board displays status of all tasks
  • E.g.: Burndown chart shows real-time sprint progress

🔎 Inspection

  • Regularly examine processes, progress, and results
  • Detect deviations or areas needing improvement
  • E.g.: 15-minute Daily Scrum every day to check progress
  • E.g.: Sprint Review to inspect the product every 2 weeks

🔄 Adaptation

  • Adjust processes and products when issues are detected
  • Change immediately, don't wait until the end of the project
  • E.g.: Retrospective finds ways to improve after each sprint
  • E.g.: Modify Sprint Backlog when new risks are discovered
✨ MODERN TRENDS

Transparency: AI dashboards automatically aggregate metrics. Inspection: AI analyzes code quality, detects technical debt. Adaptation: AI suggests improvements based on previous sprint data.

FIVE SCRUM VALUES

The Scrum Guide defines 5 core values that every Scrum team member should practice to build trust and collaboration:

🤝 Commitment

  • Commit to achieving the Sprint Goal and supporting each other
  • Not committing to finish every task, but committing to give your best effort
  • E.g.: Dev commits to completing 3 selected user stories in the sprint

🎯 Focus

  • Focus on Sprint work and the Sprint Goal
  • Avoid distractions from work outside the sprint
  • E.g.: Declining "urgent" requests unrelated to Sprint Goal

💬 Openness

  • Be transparent about progress, difficulties, and challenges
  • Willing to share both good and bad news
  • E.g.: Reporting "I'm blocked" in Daily instead of hiding problems

🙏 Respect

  • Respect each member's abilities and contributions
  • Trust the team's ability to self-organize
  • E.g.: SM doesn't give orders but trusts the team to divide work themselves

💪 Courage

  • Dare to say "no" to scope creep
  • Dare to admit mistakes and change direction
  • E.g.: Dev dares to say "this story cannot be completed in this sprint"

SCRUM FRAMEWORK - OVERVIEW

The Scrum process works in Sprint iterations (1-4 weeks):

1

Product Backlog

A list of all features and requirements to be developed. Managed and prioritized by the PO.

2

Sprint Planning

The team selects items from the Product Backlog → creates the Sprint Backlog. Sets the Sprint Goal.

3

Sprint (1-4 weeks)

The team performs the work. 15-minute Daily Scrum every day to synchronize.

4

Sprint Review & Retrospective

Demo the product to stakeholders → gather feedback. Reflect on the process → improve.

SCRUM ROLES

Product Owner (PO) — "What to build?"

  • Represents the customer, decides feature priorities
  • Manages the Product Backlog
  • Maximizes product value
  • Real-world example: PO at Grab decides whether GrabFood or GrabPay has higher priority

Scrum Master — "How to do it?"

  • Supports, facilitates, removes impediments
  • Ensures the team follows Scrum
  • NOT a project manager!
  • E.g.: SM resolves conflicts between dev and QA, negotiates server resources

Development Team — "Execute!"

  • Cross-functional team of 3-9 people
  • Self-organizing, decides how to do the work
  • Responsible for delivering the Increment
  • E.g.: Includes developer, QA, designer, DevOps in the same team

PRODUCT BACKLOG

The Product Backlog is a prioritized list of all features, requirements, and improvements to be developed:

  • Is the single source of truth — only one version for the entire product
  • Always transparent, accessible to all stakeholders
  • Higher priority items are described in more detail (Product Backlog Refinement)
  • Only the Product Owner has the authority to add, remove, or reprioritize
  • Exists permanently and is continuously updated based on feedback

Common Prioritization Techniques:

  • MoSCoW: Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have
  • WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First): value / effort
  • Kano Model: classify features by customer satisfaction
✨ MODERN TRENDS

AI analyzes user data, support tickets, app reviews to automatically suggest backlog prioritization. Jira AI can suggest story point estimation.

PRODUCT BACKLOG ITEM TYPES

User Stories

Describe features from the user's perspective

"As a buyer, I want to filter products by price so I can find items within my budget"

~60-70% of backlog items

Bugs

Defects or errors that need to be fixed

"Checkout page crashes when selecting MoMo payment on iOS 17"

Prioritized by severity: Critical > Major > Minor

Technical Tasks

Technical work not directly user-facing

"Upgrade Spring Boot from 3.1 to 3.3 to fix security vulnerability"

Supports user stories, reduces technical debt

Spikes (Research)

Research to reduce uncertainty, time-boxed

"Research integrating ChatGPT API into the current chatbot system (2 days)"

Output: knowledge, prototype, PoC

USER STORIES - IN DETAIL

Standard Format:

As a [user type], I want [feature], so that [benefit].

I.N.V.E.S.T. Criteria:

  • Independent — Independent, not dependent on other stories
  • Negotiable — Can be negotiated, not a rigid contract
  • Valuable — Delivers clear value to the user
  • Estimable — Can be estimated in story points
  • Small — Small enough to complete in 1 sprint
  • Testable — Can be tested with clear acceptance criteria

Real-World Examples:

  • E-commerce: "As a buyer, I want to see AI-summarized product reviews so I can make purchase decisions faster" (3 SP)
  • Banking: "As a user, I want to authenticate with fingerprint so I can log in securely and quickly" (5 SP)
20 MSc. Nguyen Viet Hung - CMC University

BUGS & TECHNICAL TASKS

Bugs (Software Defects)

  • Priority: Based on impact severity × number of affected users. Critical production bugs must be fixed immediately.
  • Standard Description: Steps to reproduce, Expected vs Actual result, Environment, Screenshots/Logs
  • Acceptance Criteria: Confirm bug is fixed + regression test passes
  • E.g.: "App crashes when uploading photos > 10MB on Android 14. Steps: Open app → select photo → crash. Expected: auto resize."

Technical Tasks

  • Not user-facing: Backend, infrastructure, CI/CD, refactoring
  • Specific: Clearly describe the work needed, can be estimated
  • Supportive: Serves larger user stories or reduces technical debt
  • E.g.: "Setup Docker Compose for local dev environment", "Migrate database from MySQL to PostgreSQL", "Configure CI/CD pipeline with GitHub Actions"
✨ MODERN TRENDS

Sentry AI auto-classifies bugs, suggests fixes. Copilot suggests code fixes. AI auto-generates bug reports from crash logs.

DEFINITION OF DONE (DOD)

DOD is a set of mandatory criteria that all work must meet to be considered "Done". Ensures consistent quality.

💻 Development

  • Code written following coding standards
  • Code reviewed by at least 1 person
  • No lint/compile errors

🧪 Testing

  • Unit tests pass (coverage ≥ 80%)
  • Integration tests pass
  • Manual QA testing completed

🚀 Deployment

  • Successfully deployed to staging
  • Performance test meets requirements
  • Security scan shows no Critical/High issues

✅ Acceptance

  • PO reviewed and accepted
  • Acceptance criteria 100% met
  • API/user guide documentation updated
✨ MODERN TRENDS

SonarQube AI auto-checks code quality, GitHub Actions + AI runs test suites, Snyk AI scans security. Many DOD steps can now be 100% automated with AI → teams focus on creativity instead of manual checks.

SPRINT BACKLOG

Sprint Backlog = selected items from Product Backlog + execution plan for the current Sprint:

1

Selected PBIs (Selected Items)

User stories, bugs selected during Sprint Planning. Must align with the Sprint Goal.

2

Detailed Tasks

Each PBI is broken into small tasks (2-8h). E.g.: "Design API endpoint", "Write unit tests", "Code review".

3

Sprint Goal

A clear goal for the sprint. E.g.: "Complete login module with two-factor authentication"

4

Progress Tracking

Updated daily on Scrum Board (To Do → In Progress → Done) + Burndown Chart.

✨ MODERN TRENDS

Key characteristics: Owned by the Dev Team (not PO), can be adjusted during the Sprint, always transparent. Many teams now manage Sprint Backlog directly on Jira Board or Linear.

23 MSc. Nguyen Viet Hung - CMC University

INCREMENT (PRODUCT INCREMENT)

An Increment is the sum of all completed work in the current Sprint + all previous Sprints:

📦 Potentially Releasable

At the end of each Sprint, the Increment must be in a potentially releasable state, even if the PO may not release it immediately.

E.g.: Spotify deploys to production daily but only enables features via feature flags when ready

📈 Cumulative

Each Increment builds on previous Increments, continuously adding value. Sprint 1: Login → Sprint 2: Login + Dashboard → Sprint 3: Login + Dashboard + Reports.

✅ Complies with DOD

Only work meeting the Definition of Done counts. Code that hasn't been tested = not an Increment.

💡 Delivers Value

The Increment must be a meaningful improvement to the product, not just "code is done but unusable".

✨ MODERN TRENDS

With AI-powered CI/CD (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI), every commit can auto build, test, deploy → creating continuous Increments. Feature flags + AI A/B testing enable safe releases to specific user groups.

SPRINT — THE HEART OF SCRUM

A Sprint is a container event that contains all other Scrum events. All work happens within a Sprint.

Key Characteristics:

  • Fixed duration: 1-4 weeks (most common: 2 weeks). Not changed mid-sprint.
  • New Sprint starts immediately after the previous Sprint ends — no gaps.
  • Sprint Goal: Each Sprint has 1 clear goal, serving as the "guiding star" for the team.
  • No changes to Sprint Goal mid-sprint, no quality reduction, no duration changes.
  • Scope can be renegotiated between PO and Dev Team if needed.

Sprint Cancellation:

  • Only the Product Owner has the authority to cancel a Sprint
  • Occurs when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete (E.g.: company strategy change, competitor launches similar product)
  • Very rare in practice — costly and disruptive
✨ MODERN TRENDS

With AI accelerating development, many teams shorten Sprints to 1 week. AI enables faster coding → shorter feedback loops → earlier value delivery.

26 MSc. Nguyen Viet Hung - CMC University

SPRINT PLANNING

Occurs at the beginning of each Sprint, time-boxed to a maximum of 8 hours for a 1-month Sprint (4 hours for a 2-week Sprint):

3 Questions to Answer:

1

Why is this Sprint valuable? (Why)

PO proposes the Sprint Goal. E.g.: "Complete the online payment module to increase conversion rate by 15%"

2

What can be done? (What)

The team selects PBIs from the Product Backlog based on velocity and capacity. E.g.: velocity = 30 SP → select ~30 SP of items.

3

How will it be done? (How)

Dev Team breaks each PBI into specific tasks (design, code, test, deploy). Creates the Sprint Backlog.

✨ MODERN TRENDS

Jira AI analyzes historical data to predict velocity more accurately. AI suggests task breakdowns based on similar past stories.

ESTIMATION IN SCRUM (STORY POINTS)

What are Story Points?

A unit measuring complexity (not time) of work. Combines 3 factors:

  • Complexity: Technical complexity
  • Effort: Required effort
  • Uncertainty: Level of uncertainty

Planning Poker

The most popular estimation method:

  • Uses Fibonacci sequence: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21
  • Each member picks a number independently, then discusses
  • If there's a large difference → discuss and vote again

Velocity

  • Average total story points completed per sprint
  • E.g.: Sprint 1=25, Sprint 2=30, Sprint 3=28 → Velocity ≈ 28 SP

Real-World Examples:

3SimpleAdd form validation
5MediumCreate basic CRUD API
8ComplexIntegrate payment gateway
13Very complexIntegrate AI recommendation
✨ MODERN TRENDS

Jira AI / LinearB analyzes historical data to automatically suggest story points. AI also predicts sprint completion probability based on velocity patterns. [Atlassian AI]

DAILY SCRUM (DAILY STANDUP)

A maximum 15-minute meeting every day, at the same time and place:

Each member shares:

  • What did I do yesterday? (to sync progress)
  • What will I do today? (so the team knows the plan)
  • Are there any impediments? (for SM to resolve)

Important Rules:

  • Stand up to keep it brief
  • Don't solve problems in the meeting — only identify them
  • Only Dev Team is required, SM/PO attendance is optional
  • Update Scrum Board / Burndown Chart
✨ MODERN TRENDS

Slack bot AI collects async standups, auto-summarizes progress. Geekbot, Standuply replace synchronous meetings for remote teams. AI detects blocker patterns and provides early warnings.

SPRINT REVIEW

Occurs at the end of each Sprint, time-boxed to a maximum of 4 hours. Participants: Scrum Team + Stakeholders (customers, managers, partners).

1

Product Demo

The team demos working software (not slides!) to stakeholders. Only features meeting DOD are demoed.

E.g.: Demo QR payment feature directly on the app, stakeholders try it in real-time

2

Gather Feedback

Stakeholders provide direct feedback: "Great!", "Need more filters", "UX isn't intuitive". Feedback is recorded for the PO.

E.g.: Customer requests an AI chatbot support feature → PO adds to backlog

3

Adapt the Product Backlog

Based on feedback, the PO updates the priority order of the Product Backlog for the next Sprint. Items can be added/removed.

✨ MODERN TRENDS

Use AI to analyze stakeholder feedback (sentiment analysis), auto-generate meeting notes, suggest backlog prioritization based on feedback patterns.

31 MSc. Nguyen Viet Hung - CMC University

SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE

The last event of the Sprint, time-boxed to 3 hours. Only the Scrum Team participates (no stakeholders).

3 Core Questions:

👍 What went well?

E.g.: "Pair programming sped up reviews", "CI/CD pipeline was stable"

🤔 What could be improved?

E.g.: "Sprint Planning took too long", "Testing was crammed at the end of sprint"

🎯 Specific actions for the next Sprint?

E.g.: "Apply TDD to test in parallel with coding", "Limit WIP = 2 tasks/person"

Popular Retro Techniques:

  • Start-Stop-Continue: Start / Stop / Continue doing what
  • 4Ls: Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for
  • Sailboat: Wind (helps), Anchor (hinders), Rocks (risks)

COMMON CHALLENGES & MISTAKES WHEN APPLYING SCRUM

⚠️ Common Mistakes:

  • "Scrum but..." (ScrumBut): Half-hearted adoption, skipping Retro, no clear DOD
  • SM = Project Manager: SM gives orders instead of supporting, violating self-management principles
  • Sprint = Mini Waterfall: Design week 1, code week 2, test week 3 → not Agile
  • No Sprint Goal: Sprint is just "finish the task list" without a clear objective
  • Skipping Retrospective: No improvement = no Agile
  • Backlog too large: Hundreds of items not groomed → chaos

🎯 How to Fix:

  • Follow the Scrum Guide, start correctly then customize
  • SM focuses on coaching and removing impediments
  • Each story must be cross-functional: design + code + test within the same sprint
  • Sprint Goal must be SMART: specific, measurable, achievable
  • Retro is mandatory, create 1-2 action items and follow up
  • Backlog Refinement every week, keep max 2-3 sprint items detailed
✨ MODERN TRENDS

AI analyzes sprint data to detect bad patterns: continuously declining velocity, increasing blockers, scope creep. Proactive alerts help SM act before issues become severe.
